# Enzymatic Process for Depolymerising Polyethylene Terephthalate

Synthetic sample disclosure for demonstration. Not a real invention.

- Technical field: Biotechnology and plastics recycling
- Priority date: 2018-04-30
- Jurisdiction: US

## Problem

Mechanical recycling of polyethylene terephthalate lowers material quality, and known hydrolase enzymes are too slow and too heat sensitive for industrial use on crystalline plastic.

## Solution

An engineered hydrolase with stabilising mutations is applied to amorphised plastic flakes in an aqueous reactor held near the glass transition temperature of the polymer. The monomers released are separated by membrane filtration and used to resynthesise new polymer, and the enzyme is recovered and reused.

## Key elements

E1. An engineered hydrolase enzyme comprising stabilising amino acid substitutions
E2. Pretreatment of polyethylene terephthalate flakes to reduce crystallinity
E3. An aqueous reactor operated near the glass transition temperature of the polymer
E4. Release of terephthalic acid and ethylene glycol monomers by enzymatic hydrolysis
E5. Separation of the monomers by membrane filtration
E6. Recovery and reuse of the enzyme in a subsequent batch
E7. Repolymerisation of the separated monomers into new polymer

## Optional dependent features

- The reactor pH is controlled by titration
- Colored flakes are decolorised before hydrolysis
